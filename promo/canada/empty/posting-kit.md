## Fact check
- **Area:** Canada is 9.98 million km² (2nd largest after Russia; the USA is 9.83 million km² total area).
- **Population:**
  - Canada about 41.5 million (Statistics Canada, 2025); USA about 340 million, so about 8× more.
  - California is about 39 million.
- **The border strip:** about 3 in 4 Canadians (76%) live within 160 km (100 miles) of the US border, according to Statistics Canada's 2021 census analysis. The often-quoted 90% applies to about 150 miles (240 km).
- **The territories (Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut):**
  - About 3.9 million km², roughly 39% of Canada.
  - About 130,000 people, about 0.3% of the population.
  - Nunavut is 2.09 million km² (Mexico is 1.96 million km²) with about 40,000 people.
- **Cold:**
  - Permafrost underlies roughly 40% of Canada's land (Natural Resources Canada; estimates vary with the definition).
  - −63 °C was recorded at Snag, Yukon, on 3 February 1947, the coldest temperature ever recorded in North America.
- **Land:**
  - The Canadian Shield covers about half of Canada. Its outline on the map is approximate.
  - Farmland is about 6–7% of Canada's land area.
- **History:**
  - Québec City was founded in 1608 and Montréal in 1642. Toronto was founded as York in 1793.
  - The Canadian Pacific Railway was completed in 1885.
- **Southernmost point:** Middle Island (Lake Erie) is at 41.68°N, south of the California–Oregon border at 42°N.
- **Today:**
  - The Québec City–Windsor corridor holds roughly half of Canada's population.
  - The Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver metro areas held about 35% of Canadians in 2021 (6.2M, 4.3M and 2.6M).
