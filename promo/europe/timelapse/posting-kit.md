## Fact check
- **1914:** the German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires covered much of Europe. Finland (an autonomous Grand Duchy) is coloured with Russia, Iceland with Denmark, and Malta with the UK, as they were then.
- **WW1:** the Russian (1917), German and Austro-Hungarian (1918) and Ottoman (1922) empires all fell. Poland, Czechoslovakia, the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (named Yugoslavia in 1929), Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania were independent by 1920.
- **1938:** Austria was annexed in March 1938 (the Anschluss). The Sudetenland was taken from Czechoslovakia after the Munich Agreement in October 1938. The 1938 map shows Czechoslovakia before Munich, so a ring marks the Sudetenland.
- **WW2:** Poland's borders moved west (Potsdam 1945). The USSR annexed the Baltic states (1940, and again in 1944). Germany was split into occupation zones in 1945 and into two states in 1949.
- **Cold War:** the Iron Curtain line is simplified (the inner-German border down to the Austria–Hungary–Yugoslavia tri-point). The red tint marks Warsaw Pact members.
- **1989–1993:** the Berlin Wall fell on 9 November 1989, Germany reunified on 3 October 1990, and the USSR dissolved into 15 states in December 1991. Yugoslavia broke apart from 1991 amid war. Czechoslovakia split peacefully on 1 January 1993.
- **Today:** Montenegro became independent in 2006. Kosovo declared independence in 2008 and is recognised by about half of UN members. 44 = the UN's list of European states.
- **Crimea:** shown as part of Ukraine, its internationally recognised border (UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262). Russia has occupied and claimed it since 2014.
- **Maps:** the historical borders (1914, 1920, 1938, 1945, 1960, 1994) come from the open *historical-basemaps* dataset (A. Ourednik), which is approximate. One broken outline (Switzerland, 1994) was replaced with its unchanged modern border. Countries that split during the story are shown merged until the narrator says they split. Modern borders are from Natural Earth.
