## Fact check
- **Baarle:** the Belgian municipality Baarle-Hertog includes 22 exclaves inside the Netherlands, and several of them contain Dutch counter-exclaves (the OSM data shows 22 Belgian holes in the Dutch municipality and 8 Dutch pieces within Belgian land). A building belongs to the country where its front door is.
- **Pheasant Island:** a France–Spain condominium in the Bidasoa river since the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659). Administration alternates every six months: Spain from 1 February to 31 July, France from 1 August to 31 January. It is about 200 m long and uninhabited.
- **Llívia:** under the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) and the Treaty of Llívia (1660), Spain ceded 33 villages of northern Cerdanya to France. Llívia was legally a town (*vila*), not a village, so it stayed Spanish. It is about 1.5 km from the main Spanish border.
- **Büsingen am Hochrhein:** a German municipality entirely surrounded by Switzerland (about 1,500 residents). It is in the Swiss customs area and the Swiss franc is the main currency. It has German and Swiss postcodes and phone numbers.
- **Jungholz:** an Austrian municipality connected to the rest of Austria only at a single point, the summit of the Sorgschrofen. The only road in comes from Germany.
- **Kaliningrad:** a Russian exclave between Poland and Lithuania (both EU and NATO members), several hundred kilometres from mainland Russia. It was the German city of Königsberg (East Prussia) until 1945 and was renamed in 1946.
- **Crimea:** shown as part of Ukraine, its internationally recognised border (UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262). Russia has occupied and claimed it since 2014.
- **Maps:** municipal borders for Baarle, Büsingen, Jungholz and Llívia, and the Pheasant Island outline, come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Country borders are from Natural Earth. The house and river cards are illustrations, and the Jungholz road is drawn schematically.
