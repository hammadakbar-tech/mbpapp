## Fact check
- **Same size:** Europe is about 10.2 million km² (including European Russia up to the Urals); the USA is about 9.8 million km² (including Alaska). The hook overlay moves the real US outline (including Alaska) onto Europe on an equal-area projection, so the sizes are true.
- **44 countries:** the UN's list of European states: 43 UN members plus Vatican City (the Holy See). Kosovo is drawn on the map but not counted, because it's recognised by about half of UN members. Transcontinental countries (Turkey, Cyprus, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan) are left out. Count them and you reach about 50.
- **Geography and fragmentation:** the idea that Europe's mountain barriers, long indented coastline and peninsulas favoured many competing states is a well-known argument (for example Jared Diamond's *Guns, Germs, and Steel*). Historians see it as one factor among several, which is why the video also covers language and history.
- **Languages:** the map colours each country by the language family of its main language. Belgium and Switzerland are striped because they are officially multilingual. The EU has 24 official languages.
- **Empires:** the Roman Empire map shows about AD 100 (its peak came under Trajan in AD 117). Charlemagne's Carolingian Empire is shown about AD 800, and it was split among his grandsons in 843. Napoleon's armies entered Vienna (1805, 1809), Berlin (1806), Madrid (1808) and Moscow (1812).
- **World War One:** the German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires collapsed between 1917 and 1922. Poland and Czechoslovakia (1918) and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later called Yugoslavia, 1918) appeared on the map, along with Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Austria and Hungary as separate states.
- **The 1990s:** the Soviet Union dissolved in December 1991 into 15 countries. Yugoslavia broke apart from 1991, with Montenegro independent in 2006 and Kosovo declaring independence in 2008. Czechoslovakia also split peacefully in 1993 (not covered in the video).
- **San Marino:** by tradition it was founded on 3 September 301, which makes it the world's oldest surviving republic by its own account. The video says "says", as this is a founding tradition.
- **Vatican City and Central Park:** the Vatican is about 0.44–0.49 km² and Central Park about 3.41 km², roughly 7 times bigger. The comparison is by area.
- **Crimea:** shown as part of Ukraine, its internationally recognised border (UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262). Russia has occupied and claimed it since 2014.
- **Maps:** modern borders come from Natural Earth. The 1914, 1920, AD 100 and AD 800 maps come from the open *historical-basemaps* dataset (approximate historical borders). Mountain ranges are drawn as simplified lines.
