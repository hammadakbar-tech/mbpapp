## What's new in this one: real shot changes

Every ball line is cut into **several shots** instead of one fixed layout:

- **Close-up** with a dutch tilt and slow push-in
- **Low angle**: a huge ball filling the bottom of the frame, looking down at the viewer
- **Over-the-shoulder**: the rival's shoulder huge in the foreground, the speaker behind
- **Two-shot**: both balls in frame with a slow lateral pan
- **Cutaway to the topic**: the moment a ball names something, the camera cuts to it full screen while the speaker stays in a corner. Canada's lakes light up across the map, volcanoes pop up along Japan, the coastline gets traced, the camera dives into Tokyo, Snag in the Yukon, Lake Biwa and Aomori in the snow.
- **Reaction shot**: half a second after each round is decided, a snap zoom on the loser with a shocked face and "?!"

Talking shots happen on themed sets. Canada gets an aurora, snowy forest and falling snow. Japan gets a red sun, a Fuji silhouette, a torii gate and falling cherry petals.

## Ready replies

- **"Canada has more lakes than the whole world combined!"** → Popular myth! Canada has about 62% of the world's lakes larger than 10 hectares. That's still the most of any country, but not more than everyone else combined.
- **"Japan gets more snow than Canada?!"** → For big cities, yes. Aomori averages about 6–7 m a year, Canada's snowiest big cities about 3.3–3.5 m. Canada's mountains get more (Mount Fidelity ~13.9 m), but that's not a city 😉
- **"Tokyo area isn't 37M"** → Depends on the definition. Tokyo plus Kanagawa, Saitama and Chiba is about 37 million.
- **"Canada's coastline includes islands"** → Yes, 243,042 km including all its Arctic islands. That's why it's the longest on Earth.
- **"Canadians live long too"** → They do, about 82. Japanese women lead the world at 87.3.
- **"Rigged!"** → It was 4–4 before snow. Blame Aomori ❄️
- **"Sorry"** → Canada said it first 🇨🇦😂
