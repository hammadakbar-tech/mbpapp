**How it plays:**
1. The Sun, in sunglasses, hosts the show.
2. Each planet flies up from the lineup to the stage, brags with one true fact, gets a fact card and a score out of 10, then flies back to its seat.
3. The Sun takes the crown with 99.8% of the solar system's mass (a bar chart fills the stage) and gives itself 11/10.
4. Pluto sneaks in and asks to join. "Sorry, Pluto. You're a dwarf planet now."
5. Pluto: "But… I have a heart." Everyone says "Awww" and hearts float up.
6. The Sun: "Still no." Pluto gets 0/10.
7. It ends on a YES/NO question: **should Pluto be a planet again?** That's an easy comment for everyone, and people have strong opinions about Pluto.

## Reply ideas (keep the comments going)
- "Why isn't Pluto a planet?" → *In 2006 the International Astronomical Union said a planet has to orbit the Sun, be round, AND clear its orbit of other objects. Pluto shares its zone with lots of Kuiper Belt objects, so it became a dwarf planet.*
- "Mercury is closest to the Sun, so it should be the hottest!" → *That's what most people think! Venus's thick CO₂ atmosphere traps heat (a runaway greenhouse effect), so Venus is hotter than Mercury, day AND night.*
- "Jupiter should have won!" → *Jupiter IS more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined… but the Sun is about 1,000 times more massive than Jupiter 😎*

## Fact check
- **Mercury:**
  - Its year is 88 days (87.97).
  - Mean orbital speed is about 47 km/s, the fastest of any planet.
- **Venus:**
  - Mean surface temperature is about 464–465 °C (737 K, NASA planetary fact sheet). Some NASA overview pages round it to ~475 °C, so the card says "~465 °C".
  - Venus is hotter than Mercury because its thick CO₂ atmosphere (about 92× Earth's surface pressure) traps heat.
- **Earth:**
  - The only place where life is known to exist.
  - World population is over 8 billion (about 8.2 billion in 2025).
- **Mars:** Olympus Mons is about 21.9 km high, about 2.5× Mount Everest (8,849 m). It is the tallest known volcano in the solar system.
- **Jupiter:**
  - Its volume is about 1,321 Earths.
  - The other seven planets together come to about 890 Earth volumes: Saturn ~764, Uranus ~63, Neptune ~58, and the rocky planets under 3. So by volume they all fit "with room to spare".
  - That is a volume comparison. As solid balls they couldn't be packed in perfectly, which is the usual way this fact is meant.
- **Saturn:**
  - Mean density is 0.687 g/cm³. It is the only planet less dense than water (1.0), which is the classic "float in a bathtub" fact.
  - The bathtub is a thought experiment, since Saturn is mostly gas.
- **Uranus:**
  - Its axial tilt is about 98° (97.8°), so it rolls around the Sun on its side.
  - The leading explanation is one or more giant impacts early in its history. That is not proven, hence "probably" on the card and "nobody knows exactly why" in the script.
- **Neptune:** Voyager 2 measured winds of about 2,100 km/h (~580 m/s), the fastest planetary winds in the solar system.
- **Sun:** about 99.86% of the solar system's total mass (the card says 99.8%).
- **Pluto:**
  - The International Astronomical Union reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet on 24 August 2006.
  - The "heart" is Tombaugh Regio, imaged by New Horizons on 14 July 2015. Its western lobe, Sputnik Planitia, is a huge plain of nitrogen ice.
- **Tone:** the scores (7/10, 11/10, 0/10…) and the bragging are jokes. Every fact on a card is accurate.
