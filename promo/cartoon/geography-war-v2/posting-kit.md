## What's in Version 2

- **All 67 countries as countryballs:** 23 North American and 44 European flag balls, in two armies.
- **Ten voices:** an announcer, plus a different countryball reacting after every round.
- **A GDP war** as the final round, with every ball sized by its economy.
- **A live scoreboard** that flips on each verdict.

## How it plays

**Hook (0:00–0:12)**
1. Two armies of balls charge in: "2 CONTINENTS · 67 COUNTRYBALLS · 1 WAR".
2. Close-up of Canada, USA and Mexico looming: "ONE HAS THE SIZE".
3. Cut to a swarm of 44 European balls: "ONE HAS THE NUMBERS".
4. Title: "NORTH AMERICA VS EUROPE: THE GEOGRAPHY WAR".

**The eight rounds**

| Round | Topic | Shot | Winner | Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Countries | Roll call: every ball drops into formation, 23 vs 44 | Europe | Vatican: "I'm tiny… but I still count!" |
| 2 | Size | Europe laid over North America at true size (fits ×2.4) | N. America | Canada: "Sorry, Europe. We're just… bigger, eh." |
| 3 | People | Split-screen crowds, 745M vs 617M | Europe | UK: "Rather crowded over here. We like it that way." |
| 4 | Highest peak | Side view: Elbrus rises, then Denali tops it by 548 m | N. America | USA: "Denali says hi!" |
| 5 | Visitors | Planes fly into Europe on the globe | Europe | France: "Of course they visit us. Have you seen us?" |
| 6 | Longest river | The Volga, then the Mississippi–Missouri, drawn on close-up globes | N. America | Mexico: "¡Vamos! We are level again!" |
| 7 | History | Timeline pan from Bologna (1088) to Santo Domingo (1538) | Europe | Italy: "We had universities before you had maps!" |
| 8 | **GDP war** | Europe's six biggest economies line up, then the USA ball rolls in alone | N. America | USA: "Money talks, baby!" |

**Ending:** 4–4, "A DRAW". Switzerland: "A draw? Perfect. I stay neutral." Then "Comment your side".

The tie is deliberate: both sides feel robbed, so both sides comment.

## Ready replies

- **"Europe has 50 countries!"** → *It depends who you count. 44 is the UN list of states in Europe. Turkey, Cyprus and the Caucasus are counted in Asia, and Kosovo isn't a UN member (its ball still stands with Europe).*
- **"Elbrus isn't in Europe!"** → *It depends on where you draw the border in the Caucasus. Most lists count Elbrus (5,642 m). If you use Mont Blanc (4,806 m), Denali wins by even more.*
- **"Why only six European economies in the GDP war?"** → *That's the point: the USA alone ($29T) out-earns Europe's six biggest economies combined ($17.8T). All of Europe together is about $27T; all of North America about $34T.*
- **"Russia is mostly in Asia"** → *Right, so only European Russia (west of the Urals) is counted for Europe's size.*
- **"Do Asia vs Africa!"** → *Next battle is coming. Which matchup do you want?*

## Fact check

**Round figures**

| Round | North America | Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Countries | 23 sovereign states | 44 (UN list) |
| Area | 24.7M km² | 10.2M km² (2.4× smaller) |
| Population (2024–25) | about 617M | about 745M |
| Highest peak | Denali, 6,190 m | Elbrus, 5,642 m |
| International tourist arrivals, 2024 | about 185M | about 750M (roughly 4×) |
| Longest river | Mississippi–Missouri, about 6,275 km | Volga, 3,531 km |
| Oldest university | Santo Domingo, 1538 (by tradition) | Bologna, 1088 |
| GDP (nominal, 2024) | about $34T | about $27T |

**GDP war (IMF, nominal 2024, rounded):** USA $29.2T. Germany $4.7T, UK $3.6T, France $3.2T, Italy $2.4T, Russia $2.2T, Spain $1.7T; together $17.8T. Canada $2.2T and Mexico $1.9T appear beside the USA. Each ball's area is proportional to its GDP.

**Caveats**
- These figures are from memory and rounded. I searched for the tourism figure earlier; the others I did not re-verify online, so spot-check one before quoting it in a reply.
- North America's tourist arrivals combine North America, Central America and the Caribbean.
- The Mississippi–Missouri length is quoted between about 5,970 and 6,275 km depending on the source.
- The University of Santo Domingo's 1538 founding date is the traditional one; some historians date its effective start later.
- Europe's shape uses European Russia only. Greenland is drawn with North America but isn't one of the 23 countries.
- Flag images come from flagcdn.com.
