## Reply ideas
- **"How do we know all this?"** → *In 2016, scientists drilled into the crater (IODP Expedition 364). The rock layers record what happened that first day, including charcoal from the fires.*
- **"Chickens are dinosaurs??"** → *Yep! Birds evolved from small feathered theropods, the same group as T. rex.*
- **"Could it happen again?"** → *NASA tracks nearly all near-Earth asteroids this big, and none is on a collision course. In 2022, the DART mission even proved we can nudge one off course.*

## Fact check
- **Age:** about 66 million years ago (end of the Cretaceous).
- **Size:** the impactor was about 10 km wide (estimates 10–15 km). Everest is 8.8 km tall.
- **Speed:** about 20 km/s, versus about 1 km/s for a rifle bullet, so roughly 20× faster.
- **Energy:** estimates are on the order of a billion Hiroshima bombs or more.
- **Crater:** Chicxulub, Yucatán, Mexico, about 180 km wide.
- **Earthquake:** estimated far larger than any recorded quake (estimates around magnitude 10–11).
- **Fires:** re-entering ejecta heated the upper atmosphere. Global wildfires are supported by charcoal in the crater core, although how widespread they were is still debated, so the narration says "scientists think".
- **Tsunami:** the initial wave was about 1.5 km high (Range et al., 2022), with waves of 100 m+ near the impact. The 828 m Burj Khalifa is shown for scale.
- **Drill core:** about 130 m of material filled the crater within a day, including charcoal, while sulfur was almost absent from the target rock because it had been vaporised (Gulick et al., 2019, PNAS; IODP Expedition 364, 2016).
- **Darkness:** soot, dust and sulfate aerosols blocked sunlight for months to years (an impact winter). Photosynthesis collapsed and temperatures fell sharply.
- **Extinction:** about 75% of species died out, including all non-avian dinosaurs. Birds, small mammals, crocodilians and turtles survived.
- **Map:** today's continents are shown for reference. In 66 million years the coastlines have shifted somewhat.
