## Fact check (all rounded)
- **Milky Way:** the stellar disc is about 100,000 light-years across (faint outer parts reach further). It holds about 100 to 400 billion stars, and the video says "up to 400 billion". The Sun sits about 26,000 light-years from the centre and takes about 225 to 250 million years per orbit, with 230 million the commonly quoted figure.
- **Local Group:** more than 80 known members, mostly dwarf galaxies, in a region about 10 million light-years (about 3 megaparsecs) across. The big three are Andromeda, the Milky Way and Triangulum.
- **Andromeda:** about 2.5 million light-years away and approaching the Milky Way at about 110 km/s. A merger has long been predicted in about 4.5 billion years. A 2025 study (Sawala et al., *Nature Astronomy*) put the odds of a merger within 10 billion years at about 50%. The video only says "heading our way", which is true under both.
- **Laniakea:** a supercluster mapped in 2014 by R. Brent Tully and colleagues (*Nature*). It holds about 100,000 galaxies and is about 520 million light-years across. *Lani* means heaven and *ākea* means spacious or immeasurable; the name was suggested by a Hawaiian-language professor.
- **Observable universe:** about 93 billion light-years in diameter, larger than 2 × 13.8 billion because space expanded while light travelled. Estimates of its galaxy count run from a few hundred billion up to about 2 trillion, so the video says "hundreds of billions".
- **Visuals:** the zoom, sizes, distances and screen-width ruler are roughly to scale. Galaxy shapes, dwarf galaxy positions, Laniakea's outline, its flow lines and the cosmic web pattern are artistic illustrations.

Sources: NASA (science.nasa.gov) on the Milky Way, Andromeda and the observable universe; Tully et al. 2014, "The Laniakea supercluster of galaxies" (*Nature* 513); Sawala et al. 2025 (*Nature Astronomy*); Wikipedia "Local Group".
