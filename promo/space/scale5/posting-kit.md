## Fact check (all rounded)
- **Event horizon size:** the Schwarzschild radius is r = 2GM/c², about 2.95 km per solar mass. The video shows the diameter (2r). The black disks are drawn to true scale; the orange glow and ring are artistic (a black hole's dark "shadow" in real images is about 2.6 times wider than its event horizon).
- **#1 Earth:** r ≈ 8.9 mm, so about 1.8 cm across, marble-sized. The Canadian $1 coin is 26.5 mm wide.
- **#2 The Sun:** r ≈ 2.95 km, so about 5.9 km across, less than Mount Everest's height of 8,849 m. (The Sun will never actually become a black hole; it isn't massive enough.)
- **#3 Stellar black hole:** they form when very massive stars collapse and typically weigh about 5–20 Suns. At 10 Suns, the event horizon is about 59 km across. The City of Toronto is about 43 km wide.
- **#4 Sagittarius A*:** about 4.3 million Suns, so about 25 million km across (0.17 AU). Mercury's orbit is about 116 million km across. It was imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2022.
- **#5 M87*:** about 6.5 billion Suns (EHT 2019), so about 38 billion km (about 257 AU) across. Pluto's orbit averages about 79 AU across, so roughly 3×. M87* was the first black hole ever imaged (April 2019).
- **#6 TON 618:** about 40 billion Suns (Ge et al. 2019; an older estimate was 66 billion), so about 240 billion km (about 1,600 AU) across. Neptune's orbit is about 60 AU across, so about 26 "solar systems" (planets out to Neptune) fit across it.
- **Even bigger?** Candidates such as Phoenix A may be heavier, but their masses are uncertain, so the video only says astronomers *think* bigger ones are out there.
- **Map:** the Toronto and Lake Ontario outlines come from Natural Earth. The Everest profile is stylised but drawn to the right height.

Sources: NASA (science.nasa.gov) black hole pages; Event Horizon Telescope collaboration (2019, 2022); Ge et al. 2019 (TON 618); GRAVITY collaboration (Sagittarius A* mass).
